Visa Information

Type of Visas

Visa Information

Work Visas

H-1B Visa

For workers in specialty occupations like tech, engineering, and finance. Requires a U.S. employer sponsor. Valid for 3 years, extendable to 6.

H-2A Visa

For temporary agricultural workers. Allows foreign nationals to work on U.S. farms during peak seasons.

L-1 Visa

For employees transferring within the same company from a foreign office to a U.S. office. Requires at least 1 year of employment.

Visa Information

Student Visas

F-1 Visa

For full-time academic students at accredited U.S. colleges or universities. Allows limited on-campus work and OPT after graduation.

J-1 Visa

For students, interns, scholars, or researchers in exchange programs.

M-1 Visa

For vocational and non-academic students. Covers trade schools, culinary programs, and flight academies.

Visa Information

Business & Tourism Visas

B-2 Tourist Visa

For tourism, vacation, visiting family, or medical treatment. Typically allows a stay of up to 6 months.

B-1 Business Visa

For business visitors attending meetings or conferences. Does not allow you to work for a U.S. employer.

ESTA / Visa Waiver

Citizens of 40+ countries can visit the U.S. for up to 90 days without a visa. Must apply online before travel.

Types of Green Cards

Permanent Residency Options

Family-Based Green Card

For immediate relatives of U.S. citizens including spouses, unmarried children under 21, and parents.

Employment-Based Green Card

For workers with job offers from U.S. employers. Divided into preference categories EB-1 through EB-5.

Refugee or Asylee Green Card

Refugees and asylees who have been in the U.S. for at least one year may apply for permanent residency.

Diversity Visa Lottery

Each year 55,000 green cards are available through a random lottery to people from countries with low U.S. immigration rates.

Emergency Rights

If ICE Comes to Your Door

Do not open the door. Ask if they have a judicial warrant signed by a judge. An administrative warrant does not give them the right to enter your home.

Right to Remain Silent

You do not have to answer any questions. Clearly say: "I am exercising my right to remain silent." This applies to everyone regardless of immigration status.

⚖️ Right to an Attorney

You have the right to speak with a lawyer. Do not sign any documents before speaking to one. Ask for a free legal aid organization if you cannot afford one.

If Stopped by Police

Stay calm and keep your hands visible. You are not required to answer questions about your immigration status.

H-1B Specialty Worker

Overview

The H-1B allows U.S. companies to employ foreign workers in tech, engineering, finance, and other specialty fields.

"Requires a higher education degree or its equivalent."

Key Requirements

  • A valid job offer from a U.S. company.
  • A bachelor's degree in your specific field.
  • Employer must file a Labor Condition Application.

H-2A Agricultural Worker

Overview

The H-2A visa brings foreign nationals to the U.S. to fill temporary or seasonal agricultural jobs where domestic workers are unavailable.

Key Benefits & Rules

  • Employer must provide free housing to workers.
  • Inbound and outbound transportation costs are covered.
  • Typically valid for up to 1 year (extendable to 3).

L-1 Intracompany Transfer

Overview

For international managers, executives, or specialized knowledge employees transferring to a U.S. branch of the same company.

Key Requirements

  • Must have worked for the company abroad for at least 1 continuous year within the last 3 years.
  • L-1A (Managers/Executives) can stay up to 7 years.
  • L-1B (Specialized Knowledge) can stay up to 5 years.

F-1 Academic Student

Overview

The F-1 visa is for international students who want to study full-time at an accredited college or university in the U.S.

"Must maintain a full course of study."

Work & Training

  • On-Campus Work: Up to 20 hours per week while school is in session.
  • OPT: Up to 12 months of temporary work in your field after graduation.

J-1 Exchange Visitor

Overview

For individuals approved to participate in work-and-study-based exchange visitor programs (interns, scholars, au pairs, camp counselors).

Important Note

Some J-1 visas include a 2-year home-country physical presence requirement before you can apply to change your status inside the U.S.

M-1 Vocational Student

Overview

For students attending non-academic or vocational programs, such as trade schools, flight academies, or technical training courses.

Key Limits

  • M-1 students cannot work off-campus during their studies.
  • Visas are typically issued for the length of the program up to 1 year max.

B-2 Tourist Visa

Overview

For travelers entering the U.S. for tourism, vacations, visiting friends or family, and medical treatments.

Stay Duration

Usually grants an entry stay period of up to 6 months per trip, decided by CBP customs officers at the border crossing.

B-1 Business Visitor

Overview

For short-term business travel to consult with associates, attend legal conventions, negotiate contracts, or settle estates.

"Strictly forbids performing local employment or receiving pay from U.S. companies."

ESTA Visa Waiver

Overview

Allows pre-approved citizens from 40+ specific countries to travel to the U.S. for business or tourism without obtaining a traditional visa.

Key Rules

  • Maximum stay limit of 90 days per visit.
  • Strictly non-extendable while inside the country.
  • Must register online prior to booking travel.

Family Green Card

Overview

Enables legal permanent residency sponsorship for family members of U.S. citizens and Green Card holders.

Categories

  • Immediate Relatives: Spouses, minor kids, parents (No yearly limits or queue waitlists).
  • Preference Families: Siblings, married or adult children (Subject to annual lottery limit backlogs).

Employment Green Card

Overview

Permanent residency paths based on professional skills, job offers, or business investments inside the United States.

Tier Tiers (EB-1 to EB-5)

  • EB-1: Extraordinary talents, professors, executives.
  • EB-2: Advanced degrees or exceptional professional skills.
  • EB-3: Skilled workers and professionals.
  • EB-5: Immigrant investors creating local jobs.

Refugee & Asylee Path

Overview

For individuals seeking legal protection who are unable or afraid to return to their home country due to well-founded fear of persecution.

Green Card Rule

Refugees and individuals granted asylum status are legally required or eligible to apply for a Green Card after living continuously in the U.S. for exactly 1 year.

Diversity Visa Lottery

Overview

An official annual computer-generated random drawing program that awards up to 55,000 permanent resident visas each year.

Eligibility

Open only to natives of countries with historically low rates of immigration to the United States. Requires a high school education or equivalent work history to claim.
